Before I wrote any automation, I spent two years and four months running payroll at an accounting firm in Brazil. More than 60 client companies, more than 150 people a month, legal deadlines and fines for any mistake.

Nobody there needed "AI". They needed payroll to be right, on the right day.

That environment taught me the order of things:

1. **Find where the process breaks.** Not where it is boring — where it fails. At Fluence, it was the repeated manual steps that piled up at the end of each month.
2. **Automate only that part.** Not the whole process at once. Only the step that fails or repeats, to take work out of the way of people who are already out of time.
3. **Only then think about a product.** ContMei — accounting over WhatsApp for micro-entrepreneurs — exists only because I had already seen, from the inside, which filings and reminders small-business clients forget every month.

Today, at a startup in São Paulo, the logic is the same. The automations I built there work because I first went through the operation by hand, understanding every case and every exception. Good automation is a well-understood process, written in code.

AI models have become a commodity. Knowing what to ask them — and at which point of the process — has not.
